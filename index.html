Symbiotic Infrastructure
An international scientific community

Infrastructure as a partner to nature, resources and communities.

SYMFRA advances the concept of Symbiotic Infrastructure — infrastructure systems conceived as integral components of ecological, biological and social systems, rather than structures that treat them as constraints.

Vision

Infrastructure systems are increasingly challenged by climate change, extreme weather, biodiversity loss, resource scarcity, ageing assets, rapid urbanisation, technological change and growing societal demands for sustainability, resilience and inclusion.

SYMFRA explores how nature, materials and communities can become active partners in enhancing resilience, sustainability and adaptability across the whole infrastructure lifecycle — from planning and design through operation, maintenance, adaptation and post-disaster recovery.

Work on Nature-based Solutions, bio-based materials, circular construction, infrastructure resilience, digital innovation and community engagement has largely evolved in parallel, within sectoral boundaries. That fragmentation limits the move from isolated interventions to integrated, scalable and evidence-based strategies.

SYMFRA brings these communities together across transport, energy, water, buildings, urban systems and communications to build a shared systems perspective — and a lasting international network behind it.

Topics

Four themes structure the community's work and the conference programme.

Theme 1

Infrastructure and nature

Nature-based and nature-inspired solutions, ecosystem services, biodiversity, ecological connectivity, climate adaptation, and resilience to floods, heat, drought, wildfires and landslides — supported by monitoring and digital tools.

Theme 2

Infrastructure and biological resources

Bio-based and local materials, circularity, reuse, modularity, low-carbon construction, durability, repairability and regenerative design — supported by lifecycle data and digital tracking.

Theme 3

Infrastructure and communities

Community resilience, participatory planning, governance, equity, emergency response, Living Labs and knowledge co-creation, including inclusive digitalisation and access to essential services.

Theme 4

Cross-cutting perspectives

Whole-life and systems thinking, infrastructure interdependencies and cascading risks, digitalisation, AI and digital twins, multi-hazard resilience, LCA and LCC, standards and regulation, financing, skills and scaling.

Objectives

  • Develop a shared framework and vocabulary for Symbiotic Infrastructure.
  • Connect researchers, practitioners, authorities, asset owners, industry and communities in a multidisciplinary network.
  • Integrate Nature-based Solutions, bio-based resources and community capacities.
  • Exchange methodologies for assessing environmental, technical, economic and social performance.
  • Explore the role of digital technologies in supporting integration.
  • Showcase approaches across policy, planning, financing, design, construction, operation and recovery.
  • Identify research, education and standardisation gaps and cross-sector collaboration opportunities.
  • Found a recurring conference and a wider international community.

Who SYMFRA is for

  • Researchers in civil, environmental, geotechnical, hydraulic, transport, materials, urban, ecological and social sciences.
  • Infrastructure owners and operators across transport, water, energy, public buildings and communications.
  • Public authorities, municipalities, regional administrations and civil protection agencies.
  • Engineering consultancies, contractors, technology providers, NbS suppliers and start-ups.

About the conference

SYMFRA 2027 is the founding edition of a recurring conference. It offers a cross-sector forum spanning transport, energy, water, buildings and building complexes of public interest, urban systems, communications and other critical infrastructure domains.

The conference is non-profit and committed to minimising its own environmental footprint through sustainable venue, travel, catering, materials, energy and waste practices — with impacts measured and transparently reported.

Programme

  • Technical sessionsPresentations across the four conference themes.
  • WorkshopsWorking sessions on framework, terminology and methods.
  • MatchmakingPartner-finding for projects and cross-sector collaboration.
  • Exhibition of solutionsDemonstrators from industry, research and Living Labs.
  • Social programmeEvening events and a local technical visit.

Contributions

Participation and presentation are possible with a two-page extended abstract, with open-access publication in a repository. Authors may additionally opt to publish a full-length paper of 6–12 pages in an indexed online proceedings volume.

Extended versions of selected papers will be explored for special issues of suitable journals.

Reduced fees are foreseen for early-career participants, those returning from a career hiatus, and retired participants.
